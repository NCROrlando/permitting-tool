Section 1
Purpose & Scope

This SOP establishes a consistent, repeatable process for navigating the construction permitting workflow on every Navigate project — from initial project assessment through permit closeout and final inspection sign-off.

It is designed to be used by all Navigate team members involved in project setup, coordination, and field execution. No team member should need to ask how permitting works — this document and the accompanying checklist tool are the answer.

Because permitting requirements vary by municipality in Central Florida, this SOP uses a variable framework: the core phases are universal, and municipality-specific requirements are flagged and filled in per project.

How to Use This SOP

Read this document once to understand the full process. For day-to-day project use, switch to the Project Checklist tab — enter your project details, select the municipality, and work through the interactive checklist step by step.

Section 2
Roles & Responsibilities
Role A
Project Manager / Estimator
  • Determines permit requirement during scope development
  • Identifies correct municipality and applicable codes
  • Assigns permitting lead for the project
  • Ensures permit costs are included in the project estimate
Role B
Permitting Coordinator (or assigned team member)
  • Assembles all required application documents
  • Submits application (online or in-person per municipality)
  • Tracks application status and responds to plan review comments
  • Schedules and confirms all required inspections
  • Obtains final sign-off and certificate of completion
  • Files all permit documents in project folder
Role C
Field Superintendent / Crew Lead
  • Confirms permit is posted on job site before work begins
  • Ensures inspection access and readiness at each inspection stage
  • Notifies PM immediately of any field-level inspection issues
  • Never begins work without confirmed permit in hand
Section 3
The 6-Phase Permitting Process
Phase 01
Pre-Permit Assessment
  • Confirm whether the project scope requires a permit Rule: If structural, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, or major demo is involved — it requires a permit. When in doubt, call the municipality and ask.
  • Identify the correct jurisdiction (county vs. city vs. special district)
  • Verify property ownership and confirm no existing open permits on the parcel
  • Identify all trades involved (GC, electrical, plumbing, HVAC) and confirm licensing
  • Determine if engineer/architect-sealed drawings are required
  • Add permit cost and timeline buffer to project estimate
Phase 02
Document Preparation
  • Gather required documents (see municipality-variable section below)
  • Prepare site plan or floor plan if required
  • Obtain engineer/architect drawings if required for scope
  • Prepare contractor license copy, insurance certificate, and owner authorization
  • Complete the municipality's permit application form
  • Compile into a permit application package (digital or physical per municipality)
Phase 03
Permit Submission
  • Submit application via the municipality's preferred method (online portal, email, or in-person) Variable by municipality — see variable section below.
  • Pay permit application fee
  • Record submission date, application/tracking number in Plutio project
  • Set a follow-up reminder for plan review status (typically 5–15 business days)
Phase 04
Plan Review & Approval
  • Monitor application status on municipal portal (or call if no portal)
  • Respond promptly to any plan review comments or correction requests
  • Resubmit revised plans/documents within 5 business days of receiving comments
  • Upon approval: download/collect approved permit and approved plans
  • Post permit on job site BEFORE work begins — this is a legal requirement
  • Save approved permit and plans to project folder in Plutio/Google Drive
Phase 05
Inspections
  • Identify all required inspection stages for the project scope Examples: Footings, framing, rough electrical, rough plumbing, insulation, drywall, final.
  • Schedule each inspection via municipality portal or phone before covering work
  • Ensure site and work area are ready before inspector arrives
  • Have approved plans available on-site for inspector
  • Document inspection results (pass/fail/correction) in Plutio project notes
  • If corrections required: complete immediately, document, schedule reinspection
  • Notify PM and client immediately of any failed inspections
Phase 06
Closeout & Final Inspection
  • Confirm all required inspections have passed
  • Schedule final inspection
  • Ensure all work is 100% complete before final inspection
  • Obtain Certificate of Completion or Certificate of Occupancy
  • Provide copy of CO/CC to client
  • Archive all permit documents in client project folder
  • Confirm permit is officially closed in municipal system
  • Note permit closeout date in Plutio

Section 5
Non-Negotiable Rules
Rule 01
No Work Before Permit is In Hand
  • Work cannot begin on any permitted scope until the permit is approved, issued, and posted on site. No exceptions. Starting without a permit exposes Navigate to fines, stop-work orders, and client liability.
Rule 02
Never Cover Inspectable Work Without Passing Inspection
  • Framing, rough electrical, rough plumbing, and other rough inspections must pass before insulation, drywall, or cover work proceeds. Covering uninspected work requires demolition and reinspection — a costly and avoidable mistake.
Rule 03
Open Permits Must Be Closed Before Project Closeout
  • Navigate does not hand off a completed project with an open permit. The final inspection and permit closeout are part of our deliverable to the client. Open permits affect property sales and client insurance.
